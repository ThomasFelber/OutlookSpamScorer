What a German notary does

German law requires notarial form for important contracts and declarations. The notary drafts, explains and notarises. Unlike a notary public in common-law countries, a German notary is a legally trained holder of a public office.

How it works

Four steps, the same for every matter. You sign nothing that has not been explained to you first.

  1. {{svg:telefon}}Enquiry

    You call, write or complete a questionnaire. The office clarifies what is needed and which documents are required.

  2. {{svg:feder}}Draft

    The notary drafts the contract or declaration. You receive the draft in advance to read.

  3. {{svg:urkunde}}Notarisation

    At the appointment the notary reads the deed aloud, explains it and answers questions. Then everyone signs.

  4. {{svg:haken}}Completion

    The office handles the land register, commercial register and authorities and informs you when everything is done.

What an appointment costs

Notary fees are fixed by the German Court and Notary Costs Act (GNotKG). They depend on the value of the transaction, not on the time spent, and are identical at every notary in Germany.

Advice connected with a notarisation is included in the fee. We tell you the expected costs in advance.

Explained in plain terms

Notarial deeds use words that do not occur in everyday life: Auflassung, Vollzug, Pflichtteil, Nießbrauch. At the appointment the notary explains each of them. If you want to read up beforehand, the glossary covers the most important terms in English.

Good to know

  • Bring a valid passport or identity card to every appointment.
  • If you do not understand German sufficiently, an interpreter is required by law. The office helps you find one.
  • If you cannot attend in person, you can be represented. Ask in advance.
