Gifts and transfers during your lifetime

Passing a house or other assets to the next generation while you are alive is called anticipated succession. It has to be well secured, for the person giving and the person receiving.

{{svg:schluessel}}

Typical matters

  • Parents transfer the house to a child and keep a right of residence
  • Using gift tax allowances every ten years
  • Arranging assets with a view to long-term care, bearing in mind the ten-year period
  • Balancing siblings: cash settlement or waiver of the compulsory portion

What the notary does

He drafts the transfer agreement and protects the transferor: right of residence or usufruct, care obligation, reclaim rights, for example in case of insolvency, divorce or sale by the recipient. He explains the consequences for compulsory portions, tax and social benefits and arranges registration in the land register.

Frequently asked questions

Can I reverse the gift?

Only if the contract provides for it or the law allows it, for example in case of gross ingratitude or your own impoverishment. That is why reclaim rights are set out precisely in the contract.

Right of residence or usufruct?

A right of residence allows you to live in the house yourself. Usufruct additionally allows you to let it and keep the rent. Both are registered in the land register.

Should I involve a tax adviser?

The notary points out tax consequences but does not give tax advice. For larger assets a conversation with your tax adviser before the appointment is sensible.
