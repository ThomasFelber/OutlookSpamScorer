Wills and inheritance contracts

Without a will, the statutory order of succession applies. That often does not match what people want. A notarial will creates clarity and usually spares the heirs a certificate of inheritance later.

{{svg:testament}}

Typical matters

  • Spouses want to provide for each other and appoint the children as final heirs
  • Blended family: children from different relationships
  • A business or a house should go to a specific person
  • Unmarried partners, who do not inherit by law
  • A relative with a disability should be provided for without losing benefits
  • After a death: applying for a certificate of inheritance, disclaiming, dividing the estate among heirs

What the notary does

He asks about family, assets and wishes, explains the options and their consequences, including compulsory portions and tax, and drafts the will or inheritance contract. The deed is deposited with the local court and registered in the Central Register of Wills of the Federal Chamber of Notaries, so it is found when the time comes.

Frequently asked questions

Is a handwritten will enough?

Yes, it is valid if written entirely by hand and signed. But it carries risks: unclear wording, loss, later disputes. And the heirs usually need a certificate of inheritance, which costs time and money.

Will or inheritance contract?

A will can be changed at any time by the testator alone. An inheritance contract binds both sides and can only be changed jointly. It suits situations where someone must be able to rely on the arrangement.

Can I disinherit a child?

You decide who inherits. Children, spouses and in some cases parents nevertheless keep a compulsory portion in money. It can only be waived by notarial contract.

I have assets in Switzerland or another country.

Then two legal systems come into play. The notary clarifies which succession law applies and whether a choice of law is advisable. For EU citizens the EU Succession Regulation is relevant.
