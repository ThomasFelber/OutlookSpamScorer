Buying, selling and transferring property

Every contract concerning land, a house or an apartment in Germany must be notarised, otherwise it is void. The notary ensures that buyer and seller are equally protected.

{{svg:haus}}

Typical matters

  • Buying or selling a house, a condominium apartment or a plot of land
  • Registering a land charge (Grundschuld) for bank financing
  • Transfers to children or spouses, with or without consideration
  • Dividing a building into condominium units
  • Leasehold, rights of way, pre-emption rights

What the notary does

He checks the land register, drafts the contract, resolves open points with both sides and notarises. Afterwards he supervises completion: due date of the purchase price, deletion of old encumbrances, registration of the new owner. The purchase price only becomes due once the buyer is protected.

Frequently asked questions

Who chooses the notary?

Usually the buyer, who bears the notary fees. Buyer and seller are free to agree otherwise.

When do I have to pay?

Only when the notary informs you that all conditions are met: priority notice registered in the land register, deletion documents received, municipality has waived its pre-emption right. This usually takes a few weeks.

I live in Switzerland. Is anything different?

Nothing fundamental when buying in Germany. Bring a valid passport or identity card. For financing through a Swiss bank we clarify in advance how the land charge is registered.

Do I have to attend the appointment?

You can be represented. The power of attorney is then certified afterwards. The office explains the procedure.

Does the deed have to be in German?

Yes. If a party does not understand German sufficiently, a sworn interpreter attends the appointment. A written translation can be provided on request.
